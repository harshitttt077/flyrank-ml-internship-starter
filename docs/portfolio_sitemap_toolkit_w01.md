## 2. The Lean Sitemap Architecture

Every section must earn its place against the claim and the action. There are no vanity pages, no "coming soon" dead ends, and no generic lists of skills.

```text
[ HERO / HOOK ]
  ├── Proof Statement: Systems + ML + Reactive Web
  ├── Live Availability Telemetry (Open for high-impact roles)
  └── Primary CTA Button: [Book 15-Min Technical Call]
         │
         ▼
[ THE PROOF: 3 FLAGSHIP VERIFIABLE SYSTEMS ]
  ├── System 1: FlyRank Search Refresh ML Engine
  │     └── 2.8x lift over baseline | Holdout Precision@50: 0.680 | DuckDB warehouse
  ├── System 2: Zephyr RTOS Concurrent Driver & ISR
  │     └── Deterministic state machine | Zero race conditions | Upstream PRs
  └── System 3: GeetHub / Zafran Reactive Engine
        └── Next.js App Router | Strict TypeScript Contracts | 60fps GSAP Motion
         │
         ▼
[ THE MINDSET & TOOLKIT ]
  ├── First-Principles Engineering Philosophy (Kernel/Math before abstractions)
  ├── Honest Validation Over Easy Metrics (Client holdout > in-sample fit)
  └── Core Tech Stack (Python, C/C++, TypeScript, Next.js, DuckDB, Git)
         │
         ▼
[ CONVERSION & DIRECT ACTION ]
  ├── 1-Click Embedded Calendar (15-min technical screening)
  ├── Direct Verified Email: harshitkudhial@gmail.com
  └── GitHub Code Reference: @harshitttt077
```

### Why Every Section Earns Its Place:
1. **Hero**: Answers *"Who are you and why should I care?"* in 4 seconds. Establishes the claim immediately with a direct CTA for high-intent visitors who already know what they want.
2. **Flagship Case Studies (The Proof)**: Answers *"Can you actually build it?"* Instead of an uncurated grid of 15 student projects, it presents 3 deep, production-grade systems spanning ML, systems programming, and modern web engineering with measurable metrics and GitHub proofs.
3. **Engineering Mindset**: Answers *"How do you think when things get hard?"* Shows commitment to honest measurement, error budgets, and first-principles debugging.
4. **Action (Conversion)**: Eliminates all friction. Replaces impersonal contact forms with a 1-click scheduling widget and direct email.

### What Was Deliberately Omitted (Anti-Bloat Guard):
- **No Standalone "Blog" or "Thoughts" Page**: Half-baked blog sections scream unmaintained maintenance debt. Deep technical write-ups are embedded directly as case-study deep-dives.
- **No "Skills" Progress Bars**: 90% CSS bars are meaningless. Skills are demonstrated via working repository links and metrics.
- **No Multi-Field Contact Form**: Asking for name, company, phone, and budget causes conversion friction. Direct calendar booking is immediate.

## 5. The Real Pressure-Test Prompt & Dialogue

### The Prompt Submitted:
```text
I am building my engineering portfolio around this proof statement:
"I build reliable, observable systems at the intersection of low-level performance (RTOS), applied machine learning (tabular ranking), and reactive web architecture (Next.js/GSAP)."

My intended sitemap has 5 sections:
1. Hero: Claim statement, animated particle background, 'View Work' button.
2. Flagship Systems: Deep dives into FlyRank ML engine, Zephyr RTOS driver, and GeetHub Next.js app.
3. Project Archive: A filterable grid of 12 previous side projects, student coursework, and certificates.
4. About / Musings: My personal journey into coding, favorite tech stack, and a mini-blog.
5. Contact Form: Input fields for Name, Subject, Email, Message, and a submit button.

The One Action I want is: 'Schedule a 15-minute technical call'.

Pressure-test this sitemap against my claim and my one action. What creates friction? What is distracting? What should I kill immediately?
```

### Claude's Critique (The Tutor Output):
> *"Here is your reality check: Your sitemap is working against your one action, and you are hedging your bets.*
>
> *1. Kill the 'Project Archive' immediately. A filterable grid of 12 student projects and certificates dilutes your claim. You claim to build high-performance RTOS and ML systems, yet you're showing basic CRUD apps and certificates. A senior engineering director will judge you by your weakest project. Keep the 3 flagship systems and delete the archive.*
>
> *2. Kill the 'Contact Form'. A busy founder or VP of Engineering will not type into an anonymous form and wait 3 days for an email reply. Embed a 1-click 15-minute calendar link right next to your direct email. Zero friction.*
>
> *3. Kill the 'Musings / Mini-Blog'. Unless you have 3 published, deeply original whitepapers, an empty or casual blog reads like student filler. Move your engineering philosophy directly under your 3 flagship projects.*
>
> *4. Fix the Hero CTA: Your hero button says 'View Work'—that's a passive browse action, not your One Action. If your goal is a technical call, make the call booking visible in the hero and at the footer.*
>
> *Verdict: Reduce from 5 sprawling sections to 4 tight, linear stages: Hero $\rightarrow$ 3 Flagship Verifiable Systems $\rightarrow$ Engineering Philosophy $\rightarrow$ Direct 1-Click Booking."*
